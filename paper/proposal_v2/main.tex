\begin{abstract}
Frontier work on \emph{weight-space programming} shows that LLMs can read and write the weights of other networks.
We ask the physics analogue: does an LLM hold a usable internal model of the physical world?
In a synthetic Newtonian environment with exact ground truth, we (i) measure next-state prediction in and out of distribution after LoRA fine-tuning;
(ii) probe hidden states for derived quantities with controls;
(iii) train a selective simulator-call policy under an explicit call cost;
and (iv) test whether an LLM can identify which physical law a tiny neural simulator encodes from its weights alone.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}
Reliable physical reasoning remains hard for LLMs.
We reframe the project around two headline hypotheses:
\textbf{H1}---can the model predict the next full state?
\textbf{H2}---do internals encode derived physics we can probe and teach?
Secondary questions retain the simulator-call agent (RQ3) and add a weight-reading pilot (RQ5) inspired by Takehi et al.\ (2026).

\section{Methods}
\paragraph{Environment.} 1D/2D point masses with Euler updates and event-driven elastic collisions.
Tasks: T1 next-state, T2 inverse, T3 feasibility.
ID vs OOD axes: magnitude, horizon, objects, collisions.

\paragraph{Models.} Main: \texttt{Qwen3.5-2B} with LoRA; zero-shot size sweep including stronger open models.
Probe-guided SFT adds an auxiliary linear head on mid-layer activations for $a$ and future $(x,v)$, evaluated on held-out decodability of momentum/energy.

\paragraph{Agent.} Self-error labels train a call policy; compare LLM-only, always-call, selective, random matched-rate, and oracle under $U_\lambda=\mathrm{Acc}-\lambda\cdot\mathrm{CallRate}$.

\paragraph{Weight reading.} Tiny linear/MLP one-step simulators for constant force, Hooke, drag, inverse-square; LLMs must name the law from raw weights (controls: shuffle, random init).
